# Media sources

What was found looking for the ensemble's own images, audio and video, where it
lives, and whether it can be used. Researched via Exa, August 2026.

Nothing here has been downloaded into the repo — the build environment's egress
policy blocks every host below. The URLs are exact and verified; fetching is a
job for a machine with an open connection.

---

## The short version

**There is no archive of the ensemble's own photography to recover.**
`locrianensemble.co.uk` was already a Sedo parking page in the earliest useful
Wayback capture (Dec 2015) and stayed parked through 2017; by 2024 it was a
betting-content squat, and it now serves the cockfighting stream noted in the
README. The domain has been gone for over a decade — there is no old site
behind it holding photographs.

So the photography has to come from Justin. What research *can* do is settle
what the page says, and put the ensemble's real recorded sound on it.

## Audio — usable now, no files

The ensemble's catalogue is on streaming, credited as *The Locrian Ensemble Of
London*. Verified tracks:

| Track | Album | Spotify ID |
|---|---|---|
| The Mirror and the Light | Wolf Hall: The Mirror and The Light (OST), 2024 | `5Nknri9YPCoKuvj50DSbYw` |
| The Unicorn's Horn | same | `1e9CHrtOPK9KvwCMx23VBc` |

Both credited *Debbie Wiseman, The Locrian Ensemble Of London*; © 2024 Debbie
Wiseman under exclusive licence to Silva Screen Records (SILED1768, released
13 December 2024).

**"The Mirror and the Light" is embedded in Movement IV.** An official Spotify
embed, so there is no licensing question and no audio file to host — Spotify
serves and counts the play. It is the ensemble's own sound on their own page.

Other confirmed recordings, not on the page:

- **Wolf Hall (Original Television Soundtrack)**, 2015 — the chart-topping one.
- **Mozart by Candlelight** — Chasing the Dragon `VALCDBR002`, a *live binaural
  recording made at St Martin-in-the-Fields*, Rimma Sushanskaya conducting the
  Locrian Ensemble with Fiona Kelly (flute) and Jean Kelly (harp). Eine Kleine
  Nachtmusik, the Flute & Harp Concerto, Bach's Air, Symphony No 29. Recorded
  binaurally with a Neumann head, mastered at Air Studios. This is the single
  most on-brand recording the ensemble has — the page's exact subject, captured
  live in the exact room. Worth featuring if a streaming link exists;
  I could not verify one, only physical formats (LP/CD/reel).
- **Dickensian**, Debbie Wiseman, 2016.
- **Beethoven: Contemporary Arrangements for Chamber Ensemble**, 2004 (Guild
  GMCD 7274) — with Philip Dukes.
- **Andreae: String Quartets / String Trio**, 2009 (Guild).

Apple Music Classical carries the ensemble at
`classical.music.apple.com/artist/337619505`.

## Facts worth having, all verified

Everything the page already claims checks out — including the repertoire list,
which matches a real St Martin's programme (the Bach and Vivaldi by Candlelight
concert of 8 August 2019: Handel's *Arrival of the Queen of Sheba*, Bach's Air
'on the G String', Pachelbel's Canon, Vivaldi's 'Summer', Brandenburg No 3,
Concerto for Two Cellos). These were new:

- **Justin's cello is a 1695 Francesco Ruggieri**, the Cremonese luthier. Now
  named in Movement I, where the caption previously just read "The founder's
  instrument" over a stock photograph.
- **Press.** *The Strad*: "Justin Pearson's powerful, roomy tone… stunning
  advocacy, combining lyricism of heartfelt intensity with an acute sensitivity
  to the music's harmonic progress." *The Times*: "A forthright and incisive
  soloist." Both quoted on his own site.
- **Players** who recur on the ensemble's recordings: Rita Manning and Rolf
  Wilson (violin), Philip Dukes (viola), Morgan Goff (viola), Warren Zielinski.
  Rimma Sushanskaya has conducted them at St Martin's.
- **Justin's other roles**: Principal cellist and Artistic Director of the
  National Symphony Orchestra (UK), Guest Principal of the Royal Philharmonic,
  Associate of the Royal Academy of Music (2012).

### One unresolved contradiction

**When was the ensemble formed?** Justin's own site says he formed it in
**1985**. Apple Music Classical says "Founded 1995", and a 2019 concert listing
says 1995. The page states no year, which is the safe position — but it is the
first thing to ask Justin, because a founding year belongs on a page like this.
